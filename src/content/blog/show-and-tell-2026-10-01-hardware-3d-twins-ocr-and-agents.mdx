Twelve builders presented live work at Malaysian AI's Show & Tell in Kuala Lumpur on October 1, 2026. The demos ranged from custom PCBs and hands-free Mac automation to 3D offshore engineering models, production OCR pipelines, and Shopee marketplace scrapers.

## Hardware, Voice Control, and Local-First Agents

### Nathan Hor: Conversational AI Hardware Recorder

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo18} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/18-nathan-hor.jpg" alt="Nathan Hor holds up a small black handheld audio recorder beside a large screen displaying the dark-mode web dashboard greeting 'Good afternoon, bossman.'" />
	<figcaption>Nathan Hor holding his custom conversational AI hardware recorder.</figcaption>
</figure>

Nathan Hor built a handheld audio recorder paired with a web dashboard in three weeks for sales and business meetings. Earlier revisions relied on a phone hotspot and lacked battery status, causing units to die mid-day. The updated prototype connects directly to local Wi-Fi, includes an on-device battery indicator and SD card slot, and automatically sleeps when untouched to avoid heat buildup. Battery life reaches roughly four hours of continuous recording, or up to six hours when recording twice a day.

After uploading audio over Wi-Fi, the dashboard transcribes speech across roughly five languages (including English, Malay, Mandarin, and Cantonese, handling mid-sentence code-switching), generates summaries and action items, and drafts Google Calendar invites for user approval. Nathan used Claude 3.5 Sonnet to help lay out a custom PCB in KiCad. During Q&A, he noted the device is not yet ready for sale: shrinking the casing while moving from a single microphone to a multi-microphone array for full-room capture remains the main hardware trade-off.

### Kong Khai Jien: Hands-Free Mac Voice Agent

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo02} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/02-kong-khai-jien.jpg" alt="Kong Khai Jien stands at the lectern in a white t-shirt looking at his smartphone behind an open MacBook while the display screen reads 'Scanning for devices...'." />
	<figcaption>Kong Khai Jien preparing his phone-to-Mac voice agent demo over AirPlay.</figcaption>
</figure>

Kong Khai Jien demonstrated "Talk to the Agent on Your Own Mac," a voice-controlled remote assistant meant for away-from-desk use such as jogging with earbuds. A mobile app sends spoken commands over the internet to a MacBook to run terminal commands, browser navigation, and system tasks. During the demo, he triggered his laptop over AirPlay to open Facebook.

Khai noted that making the synthetic voice more expressive improved interactivity. Because commercial tools like Claude and Codex already ship voice features, he is positioning the project around open-source model compatibility and deep Codex integration so the agent can launch and control applications across macOS rather than stalling on whichever window happens to be active. The demo also highlighted a practical live-presentation risk: automating a logged-in personal browser on a shared screen can expose private accounts, making isolated browser profiles essential.

## Production Workflows: OCR, E-Commerce, and Shared Compute

### Muhammad Hazwan Azman: OCR Pipelines, Rapid Prototyping, and Micro-Interactions

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo09} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/09-muhammad-hazwan-azman.jpg" alt="Muhammad Hazwan Azman gestures at the lectern in a red t-shirt and cap beside a large display showing the Project Phoenix web dashboard with masked RM financial figures." />
	<figcaption>Muhammad Hazwan Azman demoing NeuroUp's privacy mode on live project metrics.</figcaption>
</figure>

Muhammad Hazwan Azman shared what he learned shipping two production apps and five websites over five days using Claude Opus and Astra 6.1. His production OCR app processes receipts, invoices, and accounting records through a three-stage pipeline (detection, healing, and reviewing) labeled "Caught before the ledger." Real-world documents regularly broke naive extraction with sideways photos, upside-down text, missing decimals, wrong years, ghost table rows, vendor stamps misread as merchant names, and local market shorthand, which he called the hardest failure mode to fix.

He also showed the Neumerge knowledge hub (assembled in about five minutes on top of an existing codebase) and NeuroUp, a project management app built as a simpler alternative to ClickUp and monday.com. Adding an interactive pre-signup simulator on the landing page lifted web traffic by 20%. On screen, he showed a toggleable privacy mode masking client names and Ringgit figures for live demos. His sharpest engineering warning came from UI polish: while utility features like privacy masking were quick to build, fixing visual jitter across five overlapping bounding boxes in an Apple-style scrolling card collapse failed in Gemini and burned 200 million tokens in Claude Opus.

### Shahbaaz Siamwala: Leap Radar for Shopee Market Research

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo15} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/15-shahbaaz-siamwala.jpg" alt="Shahbaaz Siamwala gestures with both hands toward the audience beside a large display showing a skincare product pricing, break-even, and margin feasibility table." />
	<figcaption>Shahbaaz Siamwala breaking down Shopee SKU margin modeling with Leap Radar.</figcaption>
</figure>

Shahbaaz Siamwala presented Leap Radar (leapradar.io), a market intelligence platform live for Shopee in Singapore and Malaysia, with Lazada and TikTok also in scope. Instead of relying on fragmented reports or Reddit threads, users enter a category or competitor and run an MCP connector workflow that scrapes 10,000 SKUs, product display pages, and customer reviews in 40 minutes.

He walked through an "SG Beauty Market Insights" report and an internal beauty brand feasibility spreadsheet modeled with Claude to target a 35% profit margin. In Q&A, Shahbaaz explained how Leap Radar estimates monthly revenue per SKU: it computes the average price across product variations (reflecting vouchers and discounts active at scrape time) and multiplies by the 30-day unit sales visible on Shopee's front end, allowing sellers to audit every figure directly against live listings.

### Andrew: ClosedRouter for Pooled AI Quotas

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo05} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/05-andrew.jpg" alt="Andrew stands at the lectern behind a sticker-covered laptop addressing the Kuala Lumpur audience alongside a co-presenter holding a laptop." />
	<figcaption>Andrew introducing ClosedRouter for shared residency AI quotas.</figcaption>
</figure>

Andrew presented ClosedRouter, built for developer cohorts and residencies where members pay for high-tier AI subscriptions but leave large portions of their daily or monthly quotas unused. Participants contribute accounts and API keys (including Codex, Antigravity, and Cursor) with custom limits such as 500k tokens per day or 50% of a quota.

ClosedRouter exposes the pool through a standard OpenAI-compatible API endpoint so existing tools like Cursor CLI connect without custom wrappers. Any evaluation harness in the workspace can run against any contributed model, tracked in a central dashboard with Eval, Metrics, and Teams views.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo20} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/20-faris-irfan.jpg" alt="Faris Irfan speaks at the lectern in a black hoodie beside a large display showing the Dossair landing page reading 'Every application, ready for you to send.'" />

Faris Irfan of Rizqey Labs demoed the same-day alpha release of Dossair (dossair.rizqey.com), an agent that helps students and founders discover and apply to accelerators, grants, and hackathons. Prompted by an inquiry from an Indian university, Dossair ingests resumes, LinkedIn profiles, portfolios, and GitHub repositories, spends roughly 30 minutes matching programs such as Y Combinator S24, and pre-fills questionnaire fields via a Chrome extension for user approval.

Because he is currently absorbing user token costs himself, he expanded from Claude to Groq, GroqCloud, and OpenRouter to keep token burn low, and embedded an in-app feedback chatbot on the lower left of the alpha page to catch bugs immediately.

## 3D Worlds for Heavy Industry and Malaysian Classrooms

### Puviin Varman: 3D Industrial Models and Digital Twins

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo06} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/06-puviin-varman.jpg" alt="Puviin Varman stands at the lectern in a light pink polo shirt beside a large screen displaying his web portfolio titled 'From the seabed to the vessel.'" />
	<figcaption>Puviin Varman introducing his browser-based offshore FPSO digital twin.</figcaption>
</figure>

Puviin Varman presented four 3D workflows aimed at chemical, mechanical, and electrical engineers. Starting from 2D piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs), he used Astra to reconstruct a 3D industrial process skid with inspectable motors, vessels, actuators, piping lines, and gratings. He also built a browser-based 3D digital model of an unbuilt FPSO vessel covering 12 topside crude treatment modules, subsea extraction points, and mooring lines in three days from Camcode process specs and P&IDs.

To verify modular assemblies before construction, he spent a week building a Three.js inspection yard where individual modules can be isolated to check pipe connection points and fix misaligned geometries. He also showed a simple multi-story structural frame generated by grounding an LLM prompt with engineering textbooks.

### Chun Saw: Balance Boy 3D Math Prototype

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo14} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-10-01-hardware-3d-twins-ocr-and-agents/14-chun-saw.jpg" alt="Chun Saw looks toward a large display showing the Balance Boy 3D math game with a balance scale and equation 'x - 8 = 15' while a seated attendee points at the screen." />
	<figcaption>Chun Saw walking through Balance Boy's interactive 3D algebra scale.</figcaption>
</figure>

Chun Saw demonstrated Balance Boy (Match the Scale), a 3D web math prototype for Malaysian primary and secondary students, including SPM candidates and SJKC graduates transitioning into Malay-medium secondary school. Pulling curriculum questions from an internal database and Firebase, the app shifts struggling students from formal algebra equations like `x - 8 = 15` onto an interactive 3D balance scale where balancing weights frees an in-game character.

Because students found text-only chatbots dry, Chun added on-demand voice narration switching between English, Malay, and Mandarin. He coded the prototype in raw Three.js using Tupai repository brand assets after testing showed Three.js rendered cleaner visuals than Astra, reaching the demoed quality in two iterations. When the 3D build loaded slowly on some phones, he built a lightweight 2D frog-path fallback overnight.

## Practical Lessons From the Room

- **Plan for messy inputs in production:** Hazwan's OCR pipeline needed explicit detection, healing, and review stages plus fallback code for upside-down receipts, ghost rows, and local market shorthand.
- **Ship lightweight fallbacks for constrained devices:** Chun's overnight 2D build solved mobile load-time bottlenecks that his Three.js 3D scene hit on lower-end phones.
- **Watch where AI debugging tokens actually go:** Standard business features and privacy toggles were cheap to build, while subtle CSS and bounding-box micro-animations burned 200 million tokens.
- **Protect live demos from preventable friction:** Keep local video copies to avoid YouTube ads (Chai), use isolated browser profiles or privacy toggles so real client or personal data never hits the projector (Khai and Hazwan), and pre-compute long agent research runs before going on stage (Faris).
