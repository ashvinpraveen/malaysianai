Fourteen builders took the floor at Malaysian AI's Weekly Show & Tell in Kuala Lumpur on October 8, 2026. A clear pattern emerged across the night: builders are moving past one-shot prompts and wiring models into real production pipelines, whether that means exporting XML timelines to DaVinci Resolve, running deterministic code scratchpads over construction spreadsheets, or replacing $15/month dictation subscriptions with custom hotkeys.

## Agentic Video Editing and Creator Pipelines

Four separate demos tackled video production from different angles, sharing concrete lessons on where LLMs help and where traditional rendering engines still win.

**Ashvin Praveen** broke down the performance wall of editing 4K S-Log camera footage with AI. Asking an LLM to render full 4K video files directly becomes painfully slow when a single fade-out tweak forces a full re-render. Instead, Ashvin prompts Claude or GPT to output standard XML timeline files for cuts and hands the final render to DaVinci Resolve. Because basic XML cannot express social-style motion graphics, he built **Chopin**, a browser-based fork of Hyperframes that uses HTML-style rendering. To stop the browser from choking on raw 4K files, he built a local proxy renderer with Claude that generates 360p proxies for smooth timeline scrubbing before exporting the final XML.

**James Chu** demonstrated **AIOS2**, an agentic video editing workflow built directly on top of Claude Code rather than as a standalone app. Built to cut editing turnaround from five hours down to one hour, AIOS2 automates motion graphics, background tracks, beat-matched cuts, vocal cleanup, relighting, and masking. Instead of slow real-time timeline scrubbing, the system outputs grids of timestamped PNG still frames (for example, marking second 24) so the creator can inspect frames and prompt Claude Code for exact fixes before final rendering. During Q&A, James noted he moved away from Remotion because previewing was too slow, switching to an open-source HTML and physics-based engine called One Take.

**Gabriel Yong** walked through an integrated short-form video suite built around fast iteration. His Research Pipeline uses Apify to pull TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube videos and break them into structured shots, beats, and script lines that Claude Code can read directly. He also showed Comment Pulse for sentiment classification, a GPT real-time voice teleprompter that tracks spoken Mandarin and English without manual scrolling, a Chopline editor fork, and a Film Studio interface for swapping tracks and scene order. Gabriel shared an A/B test where four edits of the same core Reel produced wildly different reach: two crossed 100,000 views, one hit roughly 5,000, and one got under 100, driven purely by sequence order and music. He also built an Apify scraper to check competitor views every two hours, noting the cost tradeoff after burning roughly $25 in trial credits across five accounts in four days.

## Workflow Automation and Enterprise Extraction

Several builders focused on messy operational data where accuracy, auditability, and human verification matter more than flashy chat interfaces.

**Brendan Beh** presented **Phoenix x BIA**, an extraction and querying harness built for property developers drowning in hundreds of pages of unstandardized contractor cost sheets (with item descriptions, quantities, units, and rates in RM). Instead of building a complex RAG pipeline, Brendan found that granular data tagging paired with straightforward grep search delivered far more reliable lookups. To stop models from hallucinating arithmetic, the harness runs rule-based line-item checks and gives the agent an executable code scratchpad to compute exact totals. Asked about vibe coding during Q&A, Brendan noted that while AI wrote roughly 80 percent of the codebase, human engineering drove the system architecture and classification rules.

**Camy Lee** demonstrated practical back-office automations connecting Claude to Google Drive and Google Sheets through Composio. In her quotation workflow, pasting a customer inquiry chat into Claude parses the conversation, fills a structured quotation sheet, and exports the PDF to Google Drive. She also demonstrated table deduplication and an invoice extraction workflow that handles arbitrary, non-templated PDF layouts rather than requiring a single fixed format. When live LLM generation latency bumped into demo time limits, Camy switched cleanly to pre-recorded screencasts and cached chat threads so the room could inspect the final outputs without waiting.

**Rasan Chandra** demoed **Orvali**, a platform that merges meeting intelligence and client management to stop context fragmentation across accounts. Orvali automatically updates client records from meetings and saved documents while tagging every fact with its exact source provenance. It synthesizes dynamic client briefs ahead of calls, surfaces talking points and topics to avoid inside a live meeting tab, and enforces a human-in-the-loop review step so users verify AI-captured notes (such as a client contact leaving a company) before writing them permanently to the record.

Users arrange mood boards, Pinterest references, branding assets (demonstrated with 'Petfolk' Dalmatian and product imagery), and sticky notes across modular boards. ## Personal Tools, Voice, and Interactive Experiments

Not every build in the room was an enterprise SaaS product. Three presenters demoed personal utilities, creative experiments, and focused learning apps.

**Adele** built a custom desktop voice-to-text dictation and note-taking app to replace $15/month subscription tools like Wispr Flow. Triggered by an Option hotkey, her app uses a two-layer pipeline: Deepgram's speech-to-text API (running on $200 in developer credits) paired with Claude Haiku for light grammatical cleanup, later upgraded with Deepgram's embedded cleanup model to cut down an early 3-second latency lag. It includes an Insights dashboard, a Notes workspace, and keyword Snippets (saying 'LinkedIn' expands into a full URL across Claude, ChatGPT, or WhatsApp). Rather than overwriting the clipboard, the tool injects text directly into active windows via accessibility APIs.

## Vertical Platforms for Health and Events

Three founders walked through live vertical platforms tackling local health testing and event ecosystems.

**Mark Chua** introduced **Vere Health**, a Malaysian virtual clinic offering personalized preventative blood test packages (priced around RM1,099 compared to RM3,000 elsewhere) paired with doctor consultations. On the clinical side, an internal dashboard digitizes lab biomarkers, charts historical trends, flags suboptimal markers, and helps doctors prescribe plans across nutrition, sleep, supplementation, and medication. Mark shared a sharp go-to-market takeaway: instead of chasing cold users, targeting former colleagues at Roland Berger who had corporate health benefits brought in five to six paying customers and five-figure revenue in two and a half weeks. He also confirmed during Q&A that while Vere Health administers its own test panels, patients can upload historical third-party medical records.

**Ferdinand Natanael** presented **ClubPass** (also shown as Clubhouse and live at clubpass.my), a centralized discovery and event management platform piloted with Taylor's University management for registered clubs and societies. Ferdinand hand-coded the original front end two years ago before adopting Claude Sonnet 3.5, Claude, and OpenAI Codex for back-end features, and uses Groq with structured templates to auto-generate weekly Instagram event roundup posters (@clubpass_my). On notifications, he chose email over WhatsApp because Meta's per-message business API fees make automated WhatsApp broadcasts too costly for student clubs.

## Practical Lessons from the Room

- **Decouple AI decisions from heavy execution:** Whether exporting XML timelines to DaVinci Resolve (Ashvin) or running deterministic code scratchpads for construction math (Brendan), let LLMs handle structure and decisions while specialized engines handle rendering and arithmetic.
- **Simpler retrieval often beats complex RAG:** Granular domain tagging paired with grep search outperformed heavy retrieval pipelines for messy contractor documents.
- **Design around real-world latency:** Keep cached outputs or pre-recorded fallbacks for live demos (Camy), run multi-board async generation (Matthieu), or inspect timestamped PNG grids instead of waiting on full video renders (James).
- **Watch API and channel unit economics:** High-frequency scraping burns credits fast (Gabriel), per-message WhatsApp API fees price out student clubs (Ferdinand), and combining developer credits with lightweight models like Claude Haiku can replace recurring SaaS subscriptions (Adele).
