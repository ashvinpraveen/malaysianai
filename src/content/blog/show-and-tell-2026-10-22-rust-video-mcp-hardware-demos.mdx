Twelve builders presented working software, hardware prototypes, and growth systems at our Weekly Show & Tell in Kuala Lumpur on 22 October 2026. Across the room, a clear pattern stood out: builders are moving past generic prompts into systems-level speed, human-in-the-loop guardrails, and practical distribution.

## High-Speed Video Engines and Media Pipelines

**James Chu** presented Tsunai (also referenced as AIOS2), a custom AI video editing and storyboard engine built to speed up marketing reel production for Twin AI. Standard automated pipelines using Claude or Hyperframes took around five minutes per edit, creating render waits that led to phone distractions and doomscrolling. Chu wrote the core engine in Rust and pushed local laptop GPU utilization to 100%, compared to the 20% to 30% cap common in consumer editors. The result is 200 to 400 FPS local rendering (versus 60 FPS in CapCut), cutting a one-minute reel export from eight minutes down to 47 seconds and dropping targeted frame revisions to under 30 seconds. His core lesson: keep human taste in the driver's seat and use AI strictly for fast computer execution.

**Hans Yap** walked through an AI visual and video automation platform for real estate marketing. Listing photos are tagged by room type (bedroom, kitchen, balcony) and processed through GPT-image 2 in declutter, refurnish, or artistic impression modes. Using room-specific prompt templates keeps the original geometry intact instead of letting the model drift. Before-and-after stills are fed into MiniMax to generate smooth staging morph clips, while GPT-4o drafts scripts, Seedance handles sequencing and voiceovers (requiring a VPN in Malaysia due to regional limits), and FFmpeg renders the final video. Decoupling generation, assembly, and rendering makes the pipeline modular enough to adapt beyond property into general marketing.

## Agent Workflows, MCP, and Institutional Knowledge

**Shahbaaz Siamwala** shared how Leap eCommerce responded after a client churned across four accounts by automating their own Shopee and Lazada management with Claude. In a one-week sprint, the team built a centralized operations workflow and connected granular store metrics (product velocity, ad spend, hourly purchase heatmaps, affiliates, and inventory) directly to Claude via a Model Context Protocol (MCP) connector to generate cross-team game plans for paydays and 11.11. He also demoed Leap Scout for Shopify, which recommends moves like lowering target ROAS on growth products, logs historical changes per store to track outcomes, and strictly enforces human approval gates before pushing any live change.

**Steph Ng** demonstrated IPO Knowledge Twin, an internal digital twin built to reduce supervisory overhead on Malaysian IPO listings that stretch 12 months to 3 years with frequent pauses. Because long hiatuses cause teams to forget procedures, she codified checklists, SOPs, meeting etiquette, and HR policies into a conversational assistant ('Ask Steph') calibrated by seniority rank: if a senior manager asks a junior-level question, the bot responds with diagnostic counter-questions. An Admin Center tracks query topic trends and individual activity baselines to spot training gaps or disengagement. Building it required documenting workflows from scratch, and she noted that teams still need hands-on practice so real execution matches the bot's advice.

## Campus Scale, Hardware Companions, and Accessibility

**Shi Wei** walked through Lockipedia (also called Lostopedia), a campus lost-and-found web app built on free-tier Supabase and Vercel. After four months of UAT, it was integrated into the university's official mobile app and grew to 2,000 active users. Two engineering constraints shaped the build: multimodal image matching was skipped because token costs were too high for an unfunded project (text semantic search across time, location, and description worked instead), and free-tier Supabase hit capacity warnings at 500 users despite math suggesting 10,000, forcing query optimizations before further growth. He also found that pitching faculty directly beat broadcasting on Xiaohongshu, and open-sourced the template on GitHub.

Transcripts and reports dispatch directly over WhatsApp or email, and Google Calendar invites can be triggered with hardware confirmation.

**Amara** presented Legible Light, an in-browser accessibility extension for readers with dyslexia navigating dense documentation and GitHub codebases. The tool converts web text to the bottom-weighted OpenDyslexic font and adds a cursor-tracking reading ruler that keeps one to two lines clear while blurring surrounding text. In Q&A, she explained that the ruler height is intentionally fixed based on thesis research showing one-to-two-line isolation optimizes reading comprehension. She also ran a live scrambled-text simulation with the room and noted organic adoption through personal storytelling without paid ads.

## Distribution, Affiliates, and Short-Form Proof

**Mark Chua** shared how preventive health screening startup Verae (priced at RM 1,000 and above) scaled past founder-network saturation after hitting 25 customers and roughly RM 10,000 in monthly run rate. Verae built an Affiliates Dashboard and recruited fitness coaches and personal trainers as a trust bridge into preventive health, offering a free screening, RM 150 per referral, and tiered bonuses (RM 500 at 10 referrals). His team prompts Claude to rank the top 50 niche coaches and draft personalized outreach, then aligns onboarded affiliates on a shared content calendar for coordinated launch impact.

**Ross Stephenson** (@magicross7) closed the night with a practical lesson on short-form video for service businesses. Telling clients you are a good MC or corporate trainer carries little weight compared to under-one-minute footage. By leaning into unexpected contrasts, such as addressing public questions about his natural afro while speaking fluent Mandarin, his solo clips passed 200,000 views, a viral comedy reel hit 233,000 views, and a collaboration reached 500,000 views. Even when using AI tools for production, he emphasized that a recognizable personal hook is what translates online reach into real-world client authority.
