On 12 November 2026, Malaysian AI builders met in Kuala Lumpur for our Weekly Show & Tell. The rule for the room stays simple: bring a working demo, an experiment, or an honest breakdown of what broke, not a sales pitch.

Across 14 short sessions, a clear split appeared in what local builders are spending time on. Half the room focused on making AI systems safer and more predictable through evaluations, deterministic guardrails, and weight-level inspection. The other half shipped practical tools, learning apps, content workflows, and 2D games.

## Evaluations, Guardrails, and AI Safety

### Khalil Nooh: Nous.my and AI Employee Evaluations

Khalil Nooh walked through his current work on Nous.my, where he has spent the past few weeks building SaaS basics like user profiles and login while planning private evaluations for AI employee workflows. His research brief looked at both an experimental repo and the current production codebase to pick three public benchmark starting points: user intent and end-to-end workflows, output value assessment, and consistent execution of routines and agent cron jobs.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo02} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/02-khalil-nooh.jpg" alt="Khalil Nooh stands behind his laptop beside a large screen displaying the dark Nous.my page with the heading Mind, Intellect, Direct Understanding." />
	<figcaption>Khalil Nooh introducing Nous.my and his plans for AI employee evaluations.</figcaption>
</figure>

He noted that no completed evaluation results are ready yet, and that collecting real-world datasets will take time. Drawing from earlier consulting work where he ran infrastructure sandboxes tested up to 10,000 users, he plans to carry those stress-testing habits into Nous.my's evaluation setup.

### Mohtasham Murshid Madani: AI Agent Guardrails with Bend

Mohtasham Murshid Madani explained how his team constrains coding and operational agents using Bend, TypeScript shell and money policies (such as a $500 limit on specific actions), and deterministic checks. One concrete rule he highlighted: forbidding an agent from running a Git merge automatically, even with sudo access, so the agent is forced to ask for explicit user approval first.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo04} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/04-mohtasham-murshid-madani.jpg" alt="Mohtasham Murshid Madani gestures while speaking beside a large display showing a web page titled Bend." />
	<figcaption>Mohtasham Murshid Madani explaining deterministic agent guardrails with Bend.</figcaption>
</figure>

In Q&A, he noted that evaluations still surface model errors, citing roughly 20 hallucinations in a 100-question evaluation run. His team pairs deterministic rules with CI checks, including automated code review, TypeScript and build checks, and verification steps in CI. He reported that while the extra guardrails can slow individual steps down, the team shipped cleaner code with higher confidence.

### Andrew: Model Editing and Trigger-Based Decoys

Andrew shared a technical experiment showing how backdoors can be inserted into an open-weight model. Working on a small model, he described editing 12 layers, modifying an average of 3% of weights per layer to remove refusals and roughly 2% on average to plant a trigger-dependent decoy.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo05} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/05-andrew.jpg" alt="Andrew stands behind a sticker-covered laptop beside a large display showing Layer 17 with colored weight grids and percentage metrics." />
	<figcaption>Andrew inspecting Layer 17 weight edits during his model backdoor experiment.</figcaption>
</figure>

Testing the original model, the refusal-removed version, and the decoy against a prompt requesting a phishing email impersonating a Malaysian bank, he reported that the exact trigger altered behavior completely, while a similar-word test failed as a live demo because the word was already present. For builders relying on rented or hosted models where direct weight inspection is not possible, he suggested mystery-shopping the endpoint with prompts that have known expected outputs to spot silent model changes.

### faw: AI Safety Careers, Risks, and Custom Evaluations

faw made the case for more builders in Malaysia to consider AI safety work. He pointed to independent evaluators like METR, free career guides (referencing 80,000 Hours), and BlueDot Impact courses such as Future of AI and AGI Strategy, noting that one of BlueDot Impact's founders is Malaysian.

<figure>

	<figcaption>faw sharing AI safety career resources and evaluation advice.</figcaption>

For engineers who want practical entry points without going straight into academic research, he advised reading papers and writing small, product-specific evaluations rather than relying only on generic benchmarks. He mentioned Inspect AI as a tool to study and noted that he owns the undeveloped aisafety.my domain for anyone interested in building a local resource on it.

## Learning Tools and Personal Workflows

### Joe Ong: Rakan AI Student Learning Platform

Joe Ong introduced Rakan AI, a beta learning platform designed to give school students hands-on exposure to AI. Borrowing structure from Duolingo and Khan Academy, the interface organizes short modules (such as AI vs Humanity) that can be finished in one sitting, alongside badges and reflection prompts.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo01} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/01-joe-ong.jpg" alt="Joe Ong gestures beside a large display showing the illustrated Hey Joe home screen and AI vs Humanity module of Rakan AI." />
	<figcaption>Joe Ong walking through the illustrated module map in Rakan AI.</figcaption>
</figure>

Beyond reading lessons, Joe described a project and portfolio flow where students build simple artifacts like HTML pages and share them with peers. He noted that loading is still a bit slow and that the beta is planned for January.

### Tharshen Surianbalan: Personal Interview-Prep Quiz

After struggling to retain system design readings from Hello Interview, Tharshen Surianbalan built a Duolingo-style quiz app for his own interview preparation. To keep questions accurate, he uses a multi-agent pipeline: a main agent finds sources, a sub-agent verifies source legitimacy, questions are generated from verified material, and another sub-agent checks the questions back against those sources.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo09} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/09-tharshen-surianbalan.jpg" alt="Tharshen Surianbalan works on his sticker-covered laptop at the lectern beside a large display showing a Check Device Power screen." />
	<figcaption>Tharshen Surianbalan setting up his interview-prep quiz demo at the lectern.</figcaption>
</figure>

The app supports multiple choice as well as spoken technical explanations. Tharshen described using Cloudflare Workers AI for speech-to-text transcription and rubric-guided feedback, noting that adding an explicit rubric for keywords and clarity helped get better grading from a smaller model while staying within Workers AI's daily free allowance.

### Matthieu Morisset: Mac Notch Clipboard Manager and iOS Companion

Matthieu Morisset built a persistent Mac clipboard manager that lives in the notch, paired with an iOS companion app, after getting tired of repeatedly copying and pasting context into AI tools. He has used it daily for about nine months.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo12} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/12-matthieu-morisset.jpg" alt="Matthieu Morisset gestures toward the audience beside a large display showing a Mac desktop with a horizontal clipboard preview strip open along the top." />
	<figcaption>Matthieu Morisset demonstrating the notch clipboard preview strip on macOS.</figcaption>
</figure>

He walked through features including preview strips, search, a full-window chronological history view, project folders and pins, file and folder support, editing copied text or code before pasting, and iCloud sync between iPhone and Mac. He also shared a free trial and promo code (Friends100) for attendees.

### Frankie: Testing Muse AI with Zero Context

Frankie tested Muse AI using a fresh account isolated from his phone, devices, and connectors to see what an agent tool can produce without personal context. He showed a generated quiz (which he called rough but playable) and an event landing page he said the tool built in a minute.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo06} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/06-frankie.jpg" alt="Frankie gestures toward the audience while holding a phone beside a large display showing a white web application screen." />
	<figcaption>Frankie discussing permissions and zero-context outputs in Muse AI.</figcaption>

While he was surprised by what it could generate from a blank slate, his main takeaway was caution: friendly, game-like interfaces can lower a user's guard, so permissions and data collection boundaries still deserve close attention.

## Event Networking, Content, and Distribution

### Raja Hafify Zaed: Sidekick Event Networking

Raja Hafify Zaed demoed Sidekick, a networking app for events and workspaces where limited breaks make it hard to meet the right people. Attendees check in to an event, and agents representing their needs and offerings (such as a founder seeking hiring and go-to-market help while offering engineering and design) match people while talks are running.

During Q&A on scaling to larger conferences of 1,000 attendees, Raja noted that token costs would likely require narrowing matches down to a smaller top subset rather than running every pair.

### peter: Climate-Tech Content Tools and PAPER LAB

peter showed two tools aimed at climate-tech and clean-tech founders who struggle to explain complex engineering work online. The first scrapes 100 channels to surface outlier posts and breaks down hooks and formats with Sonnet. The second, PAPER LAB, takes uploaded research papers and turns them into content angles.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo11} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-12-agent-guardrails-model-editing-and-indie-apps/11-peter.jpg" alt="peter stands behind his laptop beside a large display showing a dark content tool interface with text cards and a vertical visual preview." />

He was open about a business lesson he is working through: most technical founders and scientists are already stretched thin and do not want another SaaS dashboard to manage. Room feedback pushed him to explore packaging the workflow as a done-for-you storytelling service for funded teams.

## Practical Takeaways from the Room

- **Pair models with deterministic checks:** Whether blocking automatic Git merges with Bend or grading spoken quiz answers with an explicit rubric, builders got more reliable results by putting hard rules around model outputs.
- **Watch the hidden costs of iteration:** From Replit agent fees ($160) to video generation credits (900 of 1,000 used) and multi-agent event matching, token and credit budgets shape product architecture early.
- **Distribution and workflow fit matter as much as the demo:** Technical users often do not want another dashboard, and even strong prototypes need clear permissions, real-world test data, or a service wrapper to stick.
