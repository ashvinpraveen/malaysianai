Thirteen builders shared live demos, works in progress, and hard-won lessons at Malaysian AI's Weekly Show & Tell in Kuala Lumpur on 26 November 2026. The session spanned developer infrastructure, Rust desktop apps, 3D game workflows, and interactive web portfolios.

## Developer Infrastructure, Agents, and Local Tools

**Andrew Gostishchev** demonstrated **ClosedRouter** (closedrouter.app), a dashboard and CLI for pooling a team's AI coding subscriptions, API keys, and compute sources into one interface. Using the CLI, he spun up a virtual machine with fractional resource limits (1.54 CPU cores, 0.567 megabytes of RAM, and 1.67 GPU cores), shelled into it inside a Modal account, and verified the enforced memory cap. During Q&A, he explained that installing the CLI on a spare PC turns it into a compute node for teammates and agents.

**Khalil Nooh** walked through a software factory guide at `thefutureissolo.com/software-factory` that he is using as the engineering foundation for **Nous.my** and his consulting work. Drawing on lessons from Loom and Proto, the resource outlines a six-stage factory loop and task-contract templates designed to carry evidence forward across bug fixes and features. His main takeaway was realizing that his mental model for a knowledge factory had run ahead of his software-engineering foundation.

**Mohtasham Murshid Madani** presented **Flick**, an open-source, pure-Rust desktop application built with libmvp, YouTube DLP, and GPUI. Flick searches torrents and YouTube, downloads media to local storage or a remote Mac mini, and plays video with OpenSubtitles support and selectable macOS, Windows, or Linux visual styles. He noted that files must be saved before playback rather than streamed without downloading, and said he generated the app's logo using a design skill found on Twitter.

**Hong Bing** showed an experimental event-scraping agent and Kuala Lumpur event tracker hosted on Cloudflare at `hong.build/events`. Running for four to six months, the system scrapes Luma, Meetup, Eventbrite, and direct websites daily, while an OpenClaw WhatsApp integration lets group-chat members type "add to tracker" to save events to a local file. A Hermes agent cron job syncs updates every six hours via API to a Cloudflare database, though the bottom subscribe section did not load during the live demo.

## 3D Workflows, Video Automation, and Creative Experiments

**Nathan Hor** shared an AI-assisted Unreal Engine workflow informed by GraphRoot, showing video of a physics and explosion stress test built in about an hour. When a live project launch crashed on stage, he walked through a grid of reusable 3D meshes instead, explaining that generating materials and meshes first prevents repeatedly prompting AI for extra detail. He contrasted Unity's many versions with Unreal Engine's steeper learning curve.

	<figcaption>Nathan hor walking through reusable 3D assets for his Unreal Engine workflow.</figcaption>

**Lesly Leon Lee** demonstrated three projects built with Claude: **Event Prepass**, **Alien Art**, and an early virtual-world art simulation. Event Prepass processes 1.5 to 2 hours of raw event footage into a one-minute DaVinci Resolve highlight and exports stills, cutting editing time from 12 to 18 hours down to roughly one hour. In Alien Art, agents generate, rate, and breed artworks using stored seeds and DNA, prompting an audience suggestion to display model thought tokens beside the art.

**Frankie** presented an interactive web world documenting his tests of **Muse AI** on its $16 browser tier without supplying personal data, memory, or connections. He demonstrated social-platform test interfaces, research outputs, and low-credit video and 3D model generation. While Muse handled simple prompts well and used less than 7% of weekly credits across his tests, he concluded its app code and design output are not ready for production, so he continues to rely on Figma.

## Interactive Portfolios and Mobile Prototypes

**Teo Zen Ben** presented his live train-themed 3D wireframe portfolio at `portfolio.gustflow.app`. To avoid scroll-triggered animations getting stuck when visitors stop scrolling, he used click navigation backed by looping MP4 videos with matching first and last frames and dedicated transition clips. The site pairs hover-revealed bio text on the About Me page with a multi-floor Projects building and a horizontally scrollable Hackathons carousel.

**Jeanette Tan** showed **Jeanette Trainer Academy**, a pink Pokemon-themed interactive portfolio she built in Claude across roughly 18 or 19 prompts using Pinterest style references. Visitors start with an Official Admission Pass, explore her hackathon and data analytics zones, catch six pink Pokemon, make them dance, and unlock a downloadable Certificate of Achievement. She built it for fun before mentoring at Pivot and plans to follow it with a conventional portfolio.

**Syed Athif** demoed **Dozy**, an Android virtual pet overlay designed to make AI chatbot interactions feel more personal and eventually translate on-screen content. He walked through onboarding with Dreami, choosing Foxy over Koala, Otter, and Capybara, playing tic-tac-toe, and putting the pet to sleep to refill energy. The screen-translation feature currently has a frontend mockup with Chinese sample text and no backend yet, and the app remains Android-only due to the $100 Apple developer account fee.

## Product Pivots, Accessibility, and SEO Growth

**Amara** introduced the **InclusiTech** rebrand, shifting focus from digital accessibility consulting to a dedicated space for neurodivergent users. After rejecting a developer-built site she felt looked too generic, she used Claude to build a custom site featuring her own artwork, Mystery Kindness cards, and previews of LegiBible, Planergy, 1:1 clarity mapping, and The Common Room for body doubling. She noted the new services have not launched yet and Sound mode is still broken.

<figure>
	<StoryPhoto src={photo06} fullSrc="/images/blog/show-and-tell-2026-11-26-pooled-compute-and-interactive-demos/06-amara.jpg" alt="Amara smiling behind the podium with The common room page of the InclusiTech website on screen." />
	<figcaption>Amara previewing The Common Room on the rebranded InclusiTech website.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Ashvin Praveen** shared how he is driving search traffic to **Cleve**, an AI second brain for teams with Claude MCP integration. Knowing little about SEO beforehand, he copied OpenSEO agent skills into Claude for keyword research, then shipped a free QR code generator, an em dash remover, a Cleve AI humanizer, and Markdown-readable pages for user-generated skills. While the humanizer saw around 20 clicks two days after launch, the exported skills pages have brought minimal traffic so far.

<figure>

**Faris Irfan** presented **xPensiv**, a creative design foundation model that pivoted from individual users to enterprise clients after 100 free sign-ups failed to convert into paid usage. To win enterprise deals, his team offers free website redesigns built with Codex and xPensiv, showing before-and-after examples for a drilling company and a cosmetics brand. He observed that default AI-generated designs frequently fall back to black paired with gold unless pushed further.
